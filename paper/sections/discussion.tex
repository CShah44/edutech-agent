\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Interpreting the Results}

The evaluation reveals that the multi-agent architecture generally outperforms single-pass prompting on both LLM-judged accuracy and automatic text quality metrics. However, the magnitude of improvement differs between metric families, raising questions about what each evaluation approach measures and values.

\textbf{Why Higher LLM Accuracy?} The multi-agent architecture achieves higher LLM-judged accuracy (+10.4\% with GPT-4.1, +7.5\% with Llama-3.3-70B) because its structured pipeline produces more comprehensive, factually grounded explanations. The breakdown agent decomposes questions systematically, the RAG retrieval provides external grounding, and the synthesis agent ensures coherent organization---all contributing to explanations that LLM judges evaluate as more accurate and complete.

\textbf{Why Higher Text Quality?} The multi-agent architecture produces more structurally consistent outputs through its explicit curation stage, leading to higher lexical overlap (ROUGE) with reference answers. RAG grounding may align generated content more closely with the factual elements present in references, improving semantic similarity (BERT-Score). The synthesis stage's explicit point curation likely yields more focused explanations that avoid tangential content, further improving overlap metrics. Finally, the four-stage refinement process---from raw facts and reasoning through synthesis to creative expression---may produce more coherent and well-organized text compared to single-pass generation.

\subsection{The Length-Quality Insight}

The output length analysis provides critical insight into the nature of multi-agent improvements. Multi-agent outputs are 67.6\% shorter yet achieve higher ROUGE scores, with a strong negative correlation ($r = -0.99$) between word count and ROUGE-1. This finding has three important implications.

First, the ROUGE gains are genuine quality improvements, not artifacts of verbosity. The multi-agent architecture produces concise, focused explanations that better match reference answers in both content and structure. Second, the higher type-token ratio (0.62 vs. 0.38) indicates more diverse vocabulary usage, likely driven by RAG retrieval that introduces domain-specific terminology not present in typical language model outputs. Third, the shorter length suggests the multi-agent system avoids tangential content, producing explanations that are more direct and focused.

This length-quality relationship has implications for evaluation methodology. Traditional concerns about ROUGE favoring longer outputs do not apply here---the multi-agent architecture achieves better scores through quality rather than quantity. Future work should consider length-normalized metrics or explicit length control in evaluation frameworks.

\subsection{Agent-Level Insights}

The multi-agent architecture's performance reveals important insights about how each agent contributes to the quality improvements.

\textbf{Breakdown Agent as Quality Driver.} The breakdown agent's role in decomposing questions into targeted components appears critical for downstream quality. Questions with well-structured breakdowns (specific search queries, clear reasoning points) consistently achieve higher ROUGE scores across all models. This suggests that the decomposition step, while adding complexity, provides essential structure that improves the quality of subsequent processing.

\textbf{Synthesis Agent as Strategy Selector.} The synthesis agent's adaptive strategy selection is a key differentiator from single-pass approaches. By evaluating retrieval quality and selecting between reasoning-heavy, facts-heavy, and balanced strategies, the agent avoids information overload and produces focused explanations. The observed distribution of strategies indicates that retrieval quality varies significantly across questions, justifying the adaptive approach.

\textbf{Parallel Analysis as Complementary Perspectives.} The parallel operation of reasoning and scientific agents provides complementary perspectives that enrich the final explanation. The reasoning agent contributes logical structure and causal understanding, while the scientific agent contributes grounded facts and citations. This parallel processing enables the synthesis agent to select from a richer pool of information than either agent alone would provide.

\textbf{Creative Agent as Accessibility Transformer.} The creative agent's role in transforming technical synthesis into accessible language appears well-suited to its specialization. By separating technical accuracy from presentation quality, the architecture allows each aspect to be optimized independently. The higher temperature (0.5) encourages natural variation that produces more engaging explanations.

\textbf{Failure Cases.} Analysis of cases where the multi-agent system underperforms reveals two primary failure modes: (1) breakdown agents that produce overly broad or vague queries lead to irrelevant retrieval and poor synthesis, and (2) synthesis agents that incorrectly assess retrieval quality select suboptimal content strategies. These failure modes suggest that improvements to breakdown quality assessment and synthesis strategy selection could further improve performance.

\textbf{Comparison with Single-Pass.} The single-pass baseline achieves comparable or lower LLM-judged accuracy, while the multi-agent system achieves higher ROUGE scores because its structured approach produces more focused, reference-aligned content. This suggests that the differences between metric families reflect different optimization objectives rather than inherent superiority of either approach.

\subsection{Practical Implications}

Our findings suggest the choice between architectures should be guided by specific use case requirements rather than universal superiority claims. Single-pass baseline approaches are preferable when: (1) questions are relatively simple and answerable from parametric knowledge, (2) low-latency response is critical, (3) LLM-judge-style accuracy is the primary evaluation criterion, or (4) minimal infrastructure complexity is desired.

Multi-agent approaches offer advantages when: (1) complex questions require explicit reasoning decomposition, (2) external grounding through retrieval is important for factual accuracy, (3) text structure and consistency (as measured by ROUGE/BERT metrics) are prioritized, (4) computational efficiency at scale matters (staged batching), or (5) conciseness and focus are valued over verbosity. Additionally, the auditable intermediate states in multi-agent systems may be valuable for debugging and quality assurance in production environments.

The length-quality finding adds a new dimension to this decision framework. When evaluating explanation quality, output length should be considered alongside traditional metrics. Our analysis shows that shorter explanations can achieve higher ROUGE scores, suggesting that conciseness and focus may be more important than verbosity.

\subsection{Future Directions}

The accuracy-quality trade-off motivates several research directions. A hybrid approach that uses multi-agent orchestration for retrieval and reasoning but feeds results to single-pass generation may capture benefits of both architectures. Fine-tuning specifically for ELI5 style while preserving multi-agent structure could potentially further improve both accuracy and text quality. Human evaluation studies comparing baseline and multi-agent outputs could clarify whether LLM judge preferences align with human preferences for simplified explanations.

The length-quality finding suggests additional directions. Investigation of explicit length control mechanisms in multi-agent systems could optimize the balance between conciseness and completeness. Development of length-normalized evaluation metrics could better capture quality improvements independent of output length. Finally, exploration of adaptive architectures that dynamically select between single-pass and multi-agent approaches based on question complexity could combine the strengths of both approaches.
