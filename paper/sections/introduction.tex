\section{Introduction}

Explaining complex topics in simple terms---the essence of the ELI5 (Explain Like I'm 5) task---is fundamental to education, science communication, and knowledge accessibility. While large language models have demonstrated impressive capabilities in generating coherent explanations, the task presents a unique challenge: maintaining factual accuracy while achieving simplicity suitable for non-expert audiences. Current approaches predominantly rely on single-pass prompting, where models must simultaneously handle fact retrieval, logical reasoning, and linguistic simplification in one generation step.

However, this monolithic approach has limitations. Smaller models often struggle with complex questions due to limited parametric knowledge, and even large models may produce hallucinations when lacking grounding in external sources. Furthermore, single-pass generation offers no explicit decomposition of the reasoning process, making it difficult to diagnose errors or ensure systematic coverage of key concepts. The trade-offs between accuracy, simplicity, and coherence in ELI5 generation remain poorly understood.

We address these challenges through a multi-agent architecture that decomposes explanation generation into four specialized stages. First, a breakdown agent decomposes questions into targeted search queries and reasoning points. Second, a parallel analysis stage combines retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) using hybrid BM25 and semantic search with explicit logical reasoning. Third, a synthesis agent evaluates retrieved facts and reasoning, selecting an adaptive content strategy (reasoning-heavy, facts-heavy, or balanced) to curate the most relevant points. Finally, a creative agent transforms these curated points into accessible ELI5 explanations. This staged approach enables explicit reasoning, external grounding through Wikipedia and curated knowledge bases, and quality-aware content mixing. A key innovation is staged batching: by processing all questions together at each stage, we reduce vLLM inference calls by approximately 100× compared to per-question processing.

To evaluate this architecture, we conduct a comprehensive comparison against baseline single-pass prompting across seven language models spanning 1B to 7B parameters (LLaMA, Qwen, Gemma, Mistral). Using 30,000 samples and eleven evaluation metrics---including LLM-based judges, ROUGE, BERT-Score, and semantic similarity---we uncover a surprising pattern. The multi-agent architecture consistently achieves lower LLM-judged accuracy (-38.2\% on average) while simultaneously improving automatic text quality metrics (+34.2\% ROUGE1, +10.0\% BERT-F1). This accuracy-quality trade-off appears across all tested models, suggesting it is architectural rather than model-specific. Despite lower judge scores, the system maintains 100\% success rate with acceptable latency (29.64 seconds average) and demonstrates computational efficiency through staged batching.

Our contributions are threefold. First, we introduce a novel multi-agent architecture for ELI5 generation featuring adaptive synthesis and efficient staged batching. Second, we provide comprehensive empirical evaluation across diverse models (14 configurations, ~66,000 total samples) and metrics, establishing benchmark comparisons for future work. Third, we discover and analyze the accuracy-quality paradox in multi-agent explanation systems, offering insights into when each architectural approach is appropriate and highlighting important questions about evaluation metric alignment. Our findings suggest that the choice between single-pass and multi-agent approaches should be guided by specific use case priorities rather than universal superiority of either method.
